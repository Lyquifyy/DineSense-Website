Start with your meal.

You don’t need a set of numbers to know what sounds right. Choose Lighter, Regular meal, or More protein. DineSense finds nearby dishes for that kind of meal.

The choice describes your meal. Never you.

Already have a target? Bring it.

Set this meal’s calories and protein, add carbs and fat if you want, or describe it in words. Save a target you use often so next time starts with a tap.

Find what fits nearby.

See dishes across the places around you, ranked for your meal. Each result explains how it fits and what to ask for, including suggested changes when the menu’s figures support them.

Published figures and estimates stay visibly different.

Or start with the place.

Explore nearby restaurants on the map, then see each place’s best dishes for your meal. Already have somewhere in mind? Look it up by name.

Missing a menu? Find it online or photograph it in the app. Those dishes are marked as estimates.

Make the order yours.

Extra chicken? Leave something off? Open Change your order to adjust the parts the menu supports. The totals and fit update as you go.

At build-your-own chains, “Build it for me” can find an everyday order for your target.

The exact words at the counter.

Your ticket lists the whole order, including every addition and anything to leave out. Its totals already include your changes.

Good decisions start with knowing the source.

A published figure and an estimate shouldn’t look like the same promise. In DineSense, they don’t.

Published, with a source.

Where a restaurant publishes nutrition, you can inspect the figures and their source. Supported ingredient changes use the restaurant’s published numbers.

Estimated, part by part.

Menus found online or photographed are estimated with their sides, sauces, and portions. Approximate figures stay marked with “~”, including after you change an order.

Menus and portions vary. DineSense is a guide to the menu; ask the restaurant about allergens and dietary requirements.

Who is DineSense for?

Anyone choosing a meal at a restaurant. Bring a meal target from a tracker, or choose Lighter, Regular meal, or More protein without setting numbers. Both lead to dishes and an exact order.

How do I try it?

DineSense is in a small, invite-only iPhone pilot through TestFlight. Email us to request access. You can include your city and iPhone/iOS version; we’ll reply about availability. An invitation isn’t guaranteed.

What do I need to use the app?

An iPhone running iOS 26 or later, a DineSense account, and DineSense Pro. Subscription plans and any eligible trial are shown in the app. During the pilot, follow the access instructions in your invitation.

Does it work with my food tracker?

You can copy a picked meal’s calories and macros into your tracker’s quick add. DineSense doesn’t automatically log meals in another app, and it keeps no food diary.

Do I have to connect Apple Health?

No. With your permission and daily targets set, DineSense can read what Apple Health says you’ve eaten today and suggest a target for this meal. Health access is optional and read-only; DineSense never writes meals to Health.

Will every restaurant have a menu?

Coverage varies. Nearby separates published nutrition, estimated menus, and places without a menu yet. You can ask the app to find a menu online or photograph one. An estimate is always labeled.

How does DineSense use AI?

Some menu, search, and dish-question features use Anthropic’s Claude. The app explains what is shared and asks for permission before use. Read the privacy policy for details.
